The transport (continuity) equation

The idea

The continuity equation $\rho_t+\dv(\rho u)=0$ carries the density along the flow of $u$. Unlike parabolic equations it never smooths: $\rho(t)$ is exactly as regular as $\rho_0$ and the flow allow. All information about $\rho$ comes from following particle paths, and the cost is paid in $\nabla u$.

Statement

Properties. Let $u$ be smooth with $u=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ and $\rho$ a smooth solution of $\rho_t+\dv(\rho u)=0$. Then:

  • Mass: $\int_\Omega\rho(t)\,dx=\int_\Omega\rho_0\,dx$.
  • Sign and lower bound: if $\rho_0\ge\delta\gt0$ then $\rho(x,t)\ge\delta\exp\big(-\int_0^t\norm{\dv u}_{L^\infty}ds\big)\gt0$; if $\rho_0\ge0$ then $\rho\ge0$, and vacuum stays vacuum.
  • Derivatives: for $q\in(3,6]$, $\norm{\rho(t)}_{H^1\cap W^{1,q}}\le\norm{\rho_0}_{H^1\cap W^{1,q}}\exp\big(C\int_0^t\norm{\nabla u}_{H^1\cap W^{1,q}}ds\big)$.

Why it is plausible

Along a particle path $\dot X = u(X,t)$, the equation reads $\frac{d}{dt}\rho(X(t),t) = -\rho\,\dv u$, so $\rho$ is multiplied by $\exp(-\int\dv u)$: this gives positivity and the lower bound. Differentiating the equation, $\nabla\rho$ satisfies a transport equation with source $-\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho - \rho\nabla\dv u$; the first term needs $\nabla u\in L^\infty$, which is why one asks for $\nabla u\in W^{1,q}$ with $q\gt3$ (Sobolev embedding), and the second needs $\nabla^2 u\in L^q$.

Where to find a proof

  • R. J. DiPerna, P.-L. Lions, Ordinary differential equations, transport theory and Sobolev spaces, Invent. Math. 98 (1989) 511–547 (non-smooth velocity fields; renormalization).
