Hölder, Young, and Lᵖ interpolation

The idea

Three inequalities do most of the bookkeeping in every estimate: Hölder splits an integral of a product into norms, Young splits a product of numbers into a sum (so that one piece can be absorbed into the left-hand side), and interpolation bounds an intermediate $L^p$ norm by two others.

Statement

Hölder. If $1 \le p, q, r \le \infty$ with $\frac1p + \frac1q = \frac1r$, then $\norm{fg}_{L^r} \le \norm{f}_{L^p}\norm{g}_{L^q}$.

Young. If $1 \lt p, q \lt \infty$ with $\frac1p + \frac1q = 1$ and $a, b \ge 0$, then $ab \le \frac{a^p}{p} + \frac{b^q}{q}$. With a parameter: for every $\varepsilon \gt 0$, $ab \le \varepsilon a^p + C(\varepsilon) b^q$, and in particular $ab \le \varepsilon a^2 + \frac{1}{4\varepsilon} b^2$ ("Cauchy's inequality with $\varepsilon$").

Interpolation of Lᵖ norms. If $1 \le p \le r \le q \le \infty$ and $\frac1r = \frac{\eta}{p} + \frac{1-\eta}{q}$ with $\eta\in[0,1]$, then $\norm{f}_{L^r} \le \norm{f}_{L^p}^{\eta}\norm{f}_{L^q}^{1-\eta}$.

Why it is plausible

The pattern to recognize: a bad term $\int |\nabla u|\,|F|$ is bounded by Hölder, then by $\varepsilon\norm{\nabla u}_{L^2}^2 + C(\varepsilon)\norm{F}_{L^2}^2$ by Young, and the first piece is moved to the left-hand side, where the dissipation $\mu\norm{\nabla u}_{L^2}^2$ absorbs it. Interpolation is how a conserved low norm (like the mass $\norm{\rho}_{L^1}$) and an energy norm (like $\norm{\rho}_{L^\gamma}$) together control the norm a term actually needs; the exponent $\eta$ is fixed by matching the reciprocals, which is just a weighted average.

Exercises

Exercise 1. Prove Young's inequality $ab \le \frac{a^p}{p} + \frac{b^q}{q}$ from the concavity of $\log$. Then deduce $ab \le \varepsilon a^2 + \frac{1}{4\varepsilon}b^2$.

Hint

For $a, b \gt 0$: $\log\big(\frac1p a^p + \frac1q b^q\big) \ge \frac1p\log a^p + \frac1q\log b^q = \log(ab)$. For the second inequality, apply the case $p = q = 2$ to the product $(\sqrt{2\varepsilon}\,a)(b/\sqrt{2\varepsilon})$.

Exercise 2. Prove the interpolation inequality $\norm{f}_{L^r} \le \norm{f}_{L^p}^{\eta}\norm{f}_{L^q}^{1-\eta}$ (for $q \lt \infty$) from Hölder's inequality.

Hint

Write $|f|^r = |f|^{\eta r}\,|f|^{(1-\eta)r}$ and apply Hölder with exponents $\frac{p}{\eta r}$ and $\frac{q}{(1-\eta)r}$. They are conjugate exactly because $\frac{\eta r}{p} + \frac{(1-\eta)r}{q} = 1$, which is the defining relation for $\eta$.

Exercise 3. Let $\rho \ge 0$ with $\int_\Omega \rho = m$, and $\gamma \gt 2$. Find $\eta$ with $\norm{\rho}_{L^2} \le m^{\eta}\norm{\rho}_{L^\gamma}^{1-\eta}$. Check that $\eta \to 0$ as $\gamma \to 2$ and explain why.

Hint

Solve $\frac12 = \frac{\eta}{1} + \frac{1-\eta}{\gamma}$: $\eta = \frac{\gamma-2}{2(\gamma-1)}$. When $\gamma = 2$ the $L^\gamma$ norm is already the $L^2$ norm, so no help from the mass is needed.

Exercise 4. Absorption. Suppose $X \ge 0$ satisfies $X^2 \le A + BX$ with $A, B \ge 0$. Show $X^2 \le 2A + B^2$. This is the step "move the term to the left-hand side" in every energy estimate.

Hint

By Young, $BX \le \frac12 X^2 + \frac12 B^2$. Substitute and subtract $\frac12 X^2$ from both sides.
