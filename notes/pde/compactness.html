Compactness: Rellich–Kondrachov and Aubin–Lions

The idea

Weak convergence cannot pass limits through nonlinear terms, so one needs strong convergence of at least one factor. Compactness theorems give it: a bound on derivatives makes a bounded set precompact in a weaker norm. In space this is Rellich–Kondrachov; in space–time it is Aubin–Lions, which needs a bound on the time derivative as well.

Statement

Rellich–Kondrachov. Let $\Omega\subset\R^n$ be bounded with Lipschitz boundary and $1\le p\lt n$. Then $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ embeds compactly in $L^q(\Omega)$ for every $1\le q\lt p^*$. For $p\ge n$ the embedding into $L^q$ is compact for every $q\lt\infty$.

Aubin–Lions (Simon's form). Let $X_0\subset X\subset X_1$ be Banach spaces with $X_0\subset X$ compact and $X\subset X_1$ continuous. Let $F$ be bounded in $L^p(0,T;X_0)$ with $\{\partial_t f: f\in F\}$ bounded in $L^r(0,T;X_1)$.

  • If $1\le p\lt\infty$ and $r\ge1$, then $F$ is relatively compact in $L^p(0,T;X)$.
  • If $p=\infty$ and $r\gt1$, then $F$ is relatively compact in $C([0,T];X)$.

Why it is plausible

A bound on $\nabla u$ prevents fast oscillation in space, and fast oscillation is exactly what weak convergence loses (the $\sin(nx)$ example on the weak convergence page has $\norm{\nabla u_n}\to\infty$). A bound on $u_t$, even in a very weak space, prevents oscillation in time. With both, no oscillation is left, and weak convergence improves to strong. The art is in finding which quantity has a time derivative bound: for compressible flow it is the momentum $\rho u$, not $u$.

Exercises

Exercise 1. Rellich in one dimension. Let $(u_n)$ be bounded in $H^1_0(0,1)$, $u_n = \sum_k a_k^{(n)}\sqrt2\sin(k\pi x)$. Show $\sum_{k\gt N}|a_k^{(n)}|^2 \le \frac{M}{N^2\pi^2}$ uniformly in $n$, and use this to extract a subsequence converging in $L^2$.

Hint

$\norm{u_n'}_{L^2}^2 = \sum_k k^2\pi^2|a_k^{(n)}|^2 \le M$. Choose a subsequence along which each coefficient converges (diagonal argument); the tails are uniformly small, so the subsequence is Cauchy in $L^2$.

Exercise 2. Why a time-derivative bound is needed. Let $w \in H^1_0(\Omega)$, $w \ne 0$, and $u_n(x,t) = \sin(nt)w(x)$. Show $(u_n)$ is bounded in $L^2(0,T;H^1_0)$ but has no subsequence converging in $L^2(0,T;L^2)$. What happens to $\partial_t u_n$?

Hint

In time this is the $\sin(nt)$ example: weak limit $0$, norm bounded below. $\partial_t u_n = n\cos(nt)w$ is unbounded in every $L^r(0,T;X_1)$.

Exercise 3. Compactness fails at the critical exponent. For $u \in C_c^\infty(B_1)$, $p \lt n$, let $u_\lambda(x) = \lambda^{(n-p)/p}u(\lambda x)$. Show $\norm{\nabla u_\lambda}_{L^p}$ and $\norm{u_\lambda}_{L^{p^*}}$ do not depend on $\lambda$, and conclude that $W^{1,p}_0(B_1) \subset L^{p^*}(B_1)$ is not compact.

Hint

Change variables. As $\lambda \to \infty$ the supports shrink to the origin, so any limit in $L^{p^*}$ would be $0$, but the norms stay equal to $\norm{u}_{L^{p^*}} \ne 0$.
