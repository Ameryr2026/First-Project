Elliptic regularity: the Lamé system and the Neumann Laplacian

The idea

For an elliptic operator, knowing the operator applied to $u$ in $L^p$ gives all second derivatives of $u$ in $L^p$. In evolution problems this is used "frozen in time": rewrite the equation as (elliptic operator)$(u)$ = (everything else), and read off two derivatives of $u$ from bounds on everything else.

Statement

Theorem (Lamé system, Dirichlet condition). Let $\Omega\subset\R^3$ be bounded with smooth boundary, $\Lame u = \mu\Delta u+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\dv u$ with $\mu\gt0$ and $\lambda+2\mu\gt0$, and $1\lt p\lt\infty$. If $u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$ and $\Lame u = F\in L^p$, then $u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)$ and $\norm{u}_{W^{2,p}}\le C\norm{F}_{L^p}$, with $C$ depending on $\Omega, p, \mu, \lambda$.

Theorem (Laplacian, Neumann condition). If $\phi\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a weak solution of $-\Delta\phi = f\in L^2$ with $\partial_\nu\phi = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$, then $\phi\in H^2(\Omega)$ and $\norm{\phi}_{H^2}\le C(\norm{f}_{L^2}+\norm{\phi}_{L^2})$. The same holds in $W^{2,p}$ for $1\lt p\lt\infty$.

Why it is plausible

On all of $\R^n$ and for the Laplacian in $L^2$ this is integration by parts twice: $\int|\nabla^2 u|^2 = \int|\Delta u|^2$. The condition $\lambda+2\mu\gt0$ is what makes the same identity work for the Lamé operator: it splits into $\mu\Delta$ on the divergence-free part of $u$ and $(\lambda+2\mu)\Delta$ on the gradient part, and both coefficients must be positive. In $L^p$ with $p\ne2$ the identity is replaced by Calderón–Zygmund theory, and near the boundary by flattening it; this is the hard part of the proof.

Where to find a proof

  • S. Agmon, A. Douglis, L. Nirenberg, Estimates near the boundary for solutions of elliptic partial differential equations satisfying general boundary conditions II, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 17 (1964) 35–92 (systems, including Lamé).
  • D. Gilbarg, N. S. Trudinger, Elliptic Partial Differential Equations of Second Order, Springer, Chapter 9 ($W^{2,p}$ estimates).
