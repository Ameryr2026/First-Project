Sobolev embeddings

The idea

Control of derivatives in $L^p$ gives control of the function itself in a better $L^q$, or even uniformly. This is how an energy bound on $\nabla u$ becomes a bound on $u$ in $L^6$, and how $\rho \in W^{1,q}$ with $q \gt 3$ gives $\rho \in L^\infty$.

Statement

Theorem. Let $\Omega\subset\R^n$ be bounded with Lipschitz boundary and $1\le p \lt \infty$.

  • If $p \lt n$: $W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)$ for $1 \le q \le p^* = \frac{np}{n-p}$, with $\norm{u}_{L^{q}} \le C\norm{u}_{W^{1,p}}$.
  • If $p \gt n$: $W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset C^{0,1-n/p}(\overline\Omega)$; in particular $\norm{u}_{L^\infty}\le C\norm{u}_{W^{1,p}}$.
  • Zero boundary values: for $u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$, $p \lt n$, the bound holds with $\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}$ alone on the right. For $n=3$: $\norm{u}_{L^6}\le C\norm{\nabla u}_{L^2}$ for $u\in H^1_0$.

In three dimensions, the cases used constantly: $H^1\subset L^6$, $W^{1,q}\subset L^\infty$ for $q\gt3$, and $W^{1,\gamma}\subset L^{3\gamma/(3-\gamma)}$ for $\gamma\lt3$.

Why it is plausible

The exponent $p^*$ is forced by scaling. Replace $u(x)$ by $u(\lambda x)$ on $\R^n$. Then $\norm{u}_{L^q}$ scales like $\lambda^{-n/q}$ and $\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}$ like $\lambda^{1-n/p}$. An inequality $\norm{u}_{L^q}\le C\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}$ can hold for all $\lambda$ only if $-\frac nq = 1 - \frac np$, that is, $q = p^*$. The same count shows where things break: one derivative in $L^p$ is worth $n/p$ "units of singularity", and when that is less than 1 the function is bounded.

Exercises

Exercise 1. Carry out the scaling argument. For $u \in C_c^\infty(\R^n)$ and $u_\lambda(x) = u(\lambda x)$, compute $\norm{u_\lambda}_{L^q}$ and $\norm{\nabla u_\lambda}_{L^p}$ in terms of $u$ and $\lambda$, and show that $\norm{u}_{L^q} \le C\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}$ for all $u$ forces $q = \frac{np}{n-p}$.

Hint

Change variables $y = \lambda x$: $\norm{u_\lambda}_{L^q} = \lambda^{-n/q}\norm{u}_{L^q}$ and $\norm{\nabla u_\lambda}_{L^p} = \lambda^{1-n/p}\norm{\nabla u}_{L^p}$. If the exponents of $\lambda$ differ, let $\lambda \to 0$ or $\lambda \to \infty$ to contradict the inequality.

Exercise 2. The one-dimensional case by hand. For $u \in C^1([0,1])$ with $u(0) = 0$, show $\norm{u}_{L^\infty} \le \norm{u'}_{L^2}$ and $|u(x) - u(y)| \le |x-y|^{1/2}\norm{u'}_{L^2}$. Compare with the theorem for $n = 1$, $p = 2$.

Hint

$u(x) - u(y) = \int_y^x u'$, then Cauchy–Schwarz. Here $p = 2 \gt n = 1$, and the Hölder exponent $1 - \frac{n}{p} = \frac12$ is exactly what you found.

Exercise 3. Sharpness in $\R^3$. Using Exercise 2 in Sobolev spaces, show that for every $q \gt 6$ there is $u \in H^1(B)$ with $u \notin L^q(B)$. So $H^1 \subset L^6$ cannot be improved.

Hint

$|x|^{-s} \in H^1(B)$ for $s \lt \frac12$, and $|x|^{-s} \notin L^q(B)$ when $sq \ge 3$. Both hold for $s \in [\frac3q, \frac12)$, which is nonempty exactly when $q \gt 6$.
