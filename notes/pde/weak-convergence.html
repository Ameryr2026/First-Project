Weak and weak* convergence

The idea

Estimates give bounds, and bounds give convergent subsequences only in weak topologies. Almost every existence proof ends with "extract a weakly convergent subsequence and show the limit solves the equation". The hard part is always that weak limits do not commute with nonlinear functions.

Statement

Definition. $u_n \weakto u$ weakly in a Banach space $X$ if $f(u_n)\to f(u)$ for every $f \in X^*$. If $X = Y^*$, $u_n \weakstarto u$ weak* if $u_n(y) \to u(y)$ for every $y \in Y$.

Theorem (compactness in weak topologies). (i) In a reflexive Banach space, every bounded sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence. (ii) If $Y$ is a separable Banach space, every bounded sequence in $Y^*$ has a weak* convergent subsequence. (iii) Norms are weakly lower semicontinuous: $u_n \weakto u$ implies $\norm{u} \le \liminf \norm{u_n}$.

Typical use: $L^p(0,T;X)$ is reflexive for $1\lt p\lt\infty$ and $X$ reflexive; $L^\infty(0,T;X)$ is the dual of $L^1(0,T;X^*)$ when $X$ is reflexive and separable, which is where the weak* limits of energy bounds live.

Why it is plausible

The standard example: $u_n(x) = \sin(nx)$ on $(0,2\pi)$ converges weakly to $0$ in $L^2$ (it oscillates faster and faster, so its integral against any fixed function vanishes), but $u_n^2 \weakto \frac12 \ne 0^2$. Weak convergence keeps averages and loses oscillations, so $\lim f(u_n) \ne f(\lim u_n)$ in general. Every nonlinear term in an equation must be handled with an extra argument: some strong convergence, or a structure that survives weak limits.

Exercises

Exercise 1. Show that $u_n(x) = \sin(nx) \weakto 0$ in $L^2(0,2\pi)$ and that $u_n^2 \weakto \frac12$, but that $u_n$ has no strongly convergent subsequence.

Hint

For $\varphi \in C_c^\infty(0,2\pi)$, integrate by parts: $|\int \sin(nx)\varphi| \le \frac1n\int|\varphi'|$. Extend to all $\varphi \in L^2$ by density, using $\norm{u_n}_{L^2} = \sqrt\pi$ for every $n$. For the squares use $\sin^2(nx) = \frac12 - \frac12\cos(2nx)$. A strong limit would have to be the weak limit $0$, but the norms stay $\sqrt\pi$.

Exercise 2. In a Hilbert space, prove from the definition that $u_n \weakto u$ implies $\norm{u} \le \liminf\norm{u_n}$. Give an example where the inequality is strict.

Hint

$\norm{u}^2 = \lim (u_n, u) \le \liminf \norm{u_n}\,\norm{u}$. The sequence of Exercise 1 is an example.

Exercise 3. In a Hilbert space, show that $u_n \weakto u$ together with $\norm{u_n} \to \norm{u}$ implies $u_n \to u$ strongly. This is the pattern behind every "weak convergence plus convergence of the norm" argument.

Hint

Expand $\norm{u_n - u}^2 = \norm{u_n}^2 - 2(u_n, u) + \norm{u}^2$ and let $n \to \infty$.
