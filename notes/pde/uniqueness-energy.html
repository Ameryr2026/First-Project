Uniqueness by difference estimates

The idea

To show two solutions with the same data coincide, subtract them and estimate the difference. The trick is to measure the difference in a weaker norm than the solutions themselves live in, so that the regularity of the two solutions can be used freely as coefficients.

Statement

The scheme.

  1. Write the equations for the difference $(\bar\rho,\bar u,\bar\phi)$ of two solutions. Nonlinear terms become linear in the difference, with coefficients built from the two solutions.
  2. Run an energy estimate for the difference at a low level (typically $L^2$), producing $\frac{d}{dt}Y + \text{dissipation}\le K(t)\,Y$.
  3. Check $K\in L^1(0,T)$ using the regularity of the solutions, and apply Gronwall with $Y(0)=0$.

A variant: weak–strong uniqueness compares a weak solution with a strong one with the same data, using the energy inequality of the weak solution in place of the equation, and shows they coincide.

Why it is plausible

Uniqueness asks less than existence: one does not need new bounds, only that the bounds already available are strong enough to control the coefficient $K(t)$. That is why uniqueness theorems are usually stated in the same class where existence of strong solutions was proved, and why uniqueness of weak solutions is often open.

Where to find a proof

  • L. C. Evans, Partial Differential Equations, 2nd ed., AMS, 2010, §7.1.2 (Theorem 4, uniqueness for linear parabolic equations).
  • X. Huo, A. Jüngel, Global existence and weak-strong uniqueness for chemotaxis compressible Navier–Stokes equations modeling vascular network formation, J. Math. Fluid Mech. 26 (2024) 11.
