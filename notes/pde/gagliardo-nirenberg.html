Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities

The idea

A refinement of Sobolev embedding: an intermediate norm is bounded by a product of a high norm and a low norm, with powers. This is what lets a nonlinear term be split so that the high norm appears with a power below 2 and can be absorbed by Young's inequality.

Statement

Theorem (on $\R^n$). Let $1\le q, r\le\infty$, $j \lt m$ integers, and $\frac{j}{m}\le a\le 1$ with

\[ \frac1p = \frac jn + a\Big(\frac1r - \frac mn\Big) + \frac{1-a}{q}. \]

Then $\norm{\nabla^j u}_{L^p}\le C\norm{\nabla^m u}_{L^r}^{a}\norm{u}_{L^q}^{1-a}$, except that $a = 1$ is not allowed when $1\lt r\lt\infty$ and $m - j - n/r$ is a nonnegative integer. On a bounded Lipschitz domain the same holds with an extra lower-order term, e.g. $+\,C\norm{u}_{L^q}$, on the right.

The form used most in 3D. For $f\in H^1(\Omega)$, $\Omega\subset\R^3$ bounded and Lipschitz: $\norm{f}_{L^3}\le C\norm{f}_{L^2}^{1/2}\norm{f}_{H^1}^{1/2}$. Applied to $f=\nabla u$: $\norm{\nabla u}_{L^3}\le C\norm{\nabla u}_{L^2}^{1/2}\norm{\nabla u}_{H^1}^{1/2}$.

Why it is plausible

The example is just two facts in a row: interpolate $L^3$ between $L^2$ and $L^6$ (the weights are $\frac12,\frac12$ because $\frac13 = \frac{1/2}{2} + \frac{1/2}{6}$), then bound $L^6$ by $H^1$ with Sobolev. The general exponent relation is again forced by scaling, exactly as for Sobolev embedding.

Where to find a proof
