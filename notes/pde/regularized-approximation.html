Regularized approximation schemes

The idea

When a system is too degenerate to solve directly, add small terms that make it solvable, prove estimates uniform in the small parameters, and remove the terms one at a time. Each added term must keep the energy structure intact, otherwise the uniform estimates are lost. Designing the regularization is often where the new idea of a paper lives.

Statement

The standard scheme for compressible Navier–Stokes (Feireisl–Novotný–Petzeltová).

\[ \begin{gathered} \rho_t+\dv(\rho u)=\varepsilon\Delta\rho,\\ (\rho u)_t+\dv(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p(\rho)+\delta\nabla\rho^\beta+\varepsilon\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla u =\mu\Delta u+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\dv u, \end{gathered} \]
  • $\varepsilon\Delta\rho$ (artificial viscosity) makes the continuity equation parabolic: $\rho$ becomes smooth and positive.
  • $\varepsilon\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla u$ cancels the energy produced by $\varepsilon\Delta\rho$, so the energy law survives.
  • $\delta\rho^\beta$ with $\beta$ large (artificial pressure) gives extra integrability of $\rho$ for the intermediate limits.

Limits are taken in order: Galerkin index $\to\infty$, then $\varepsilon\to0$, then $\delta\to0$.

Why it is plausible

Each regularization fixes one defect and costs one limit. The order matters: $\varepsilon\to0$ is done while $\delta\rho^\beta$ still provides integrability, and $\delta\to0$ last, when only the genuine pressure is left. In a coupled system, a regularization of one equation can break the energy balance with another, and then a new term must be invented to restore it.

Where to find a proof

  • E. Feireisl, A. Novotný, H. Petzeltová, On the existence of globally defined weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations, J. Math. Fluid Mech. 3 (2001) 358–392.
  • A. Novotný, I. Straškraba, Introduction to the Mathematical Theory of Compressible Flow, Oxford, 2004, Chapter 7.
