Problems in Analysis

Dat Tran, Joey Beauvais-Feisthauer, Khoa Vu, Amer Rawashdeh

These are problems in real and complex analysis that we worked through together during our graduate studies at Wayne State University, with our solutions. The problems are grouped by topic. The first section collects the standard results that the solutions use; try each problem before you open its solution.

Written by Dat Tran (tranbadat@wayne.edu), Joey Beauvais-Feisthauer (joeybf@wayne.edu), Khoa Vu (khoavu@wayne.edu) and Amer Rawashdeh. If you find a mistake, please write to one of us.

Printable version: PDF (all solutions printed) · LaTeX source.

Topics

  • 1. Background
    The standard inequalities, convergence tests and theorems of real and complex analysis that the solutions use.
  • 2. Sequences and limits · 6 problems
    Recursive sequences: bounded and monotone, then pass to the limit in the recursion.
  • 3. Series of numbers · 7 problems
    Comparison through AM-GM and Cauchy-Schwarz, partial sums and tails, and summation by parts.
  • 4. Continuity, derivatives and the mean value theorem · 14 problems
    Uniform continuity, functional equations, and inequalities between a function and its derivative.
  • 5. The Riemann integral and integral inequalities · 7 problems
    Darboux sums, integrals depending on a parameter, recursions for integrals, Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen.
  • 6. Sequences and series of functions · 9 problems
    Uniform convergence and what it preserves, equicontinuity, and the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem.
  • 7. Metric spaces and topology · 11 problems
    Compactness, connectedness, completeness, dense and perfect sets.
  • 8. Functions of several variables · 6 problems
    Differentiability, double integrals, the divergence theorem, and the implicit and inverse function theorems.
  • 9. Real integrals by residues · 11 problems
    Trigonometric integrals on the unit circle, half-circle contours with Jordan's lemma, and branch cuts.
  • 10. Analytic functions · 15 problems
    Cauchy's estimates, Liouville, the maximum modulus principle, Schwarz's lemma, residues and harmonic functions.
