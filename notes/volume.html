Volumes of Revolution

Amer Rawashdeh · October 2026

Take the region under the graph of $y = f(x)$ for $a \le x \le b$ and turn it once about the $x$-axis. It sweeps out a solid of revolution. To find its volume, cut $[a,b]$ into $n$ pieces of width $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}$ with midpoints $m_1, \dots, m_n$. Over each piece the solid is close to a disc: a short cylinder of radius $f(m_i)$ and thickness $\Delta x$, with volume $\pi f(m_i)^2\,\Delta x$. Adding the discs,

\[ V_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pi f(m_i)^2\,\Delta x. \]

This is a Riemann sum for the function $\pi f(x)^2$ (see Riemann sums), so as $n \to \infty$ it approaches the integral:

\[ V = \pi \int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx. \]

Try it

Enter a function and an interval. Press ▶ next to the angle (or move the slider) to turn the region; the flat face where the solid is cut shows its cross-section, the region itself. Drag the picture to look at the solid from any side, and zoom with the mouse wheel or two fingers.

An example

For $f(x) = x$ on $[0,3]$ (the starting example above), the region is a triangle and the solid is a cone of radius $3$ and height $3$:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^3 x^2\,dx = 9\pi \approx 28.27, \]

the same as the cone formula $\frac13 \pi r^2 h$. Try $\sqrt{x}$ on $[0,4]$ next: the discs give the exact volume $8\pi$ for every $n$. Can you explain why?

Between two curves, about any horizontal line

The same idea works for the region between two curves $g(x) \le y \le f(x)$, turned about a horizontal line $y = c$. Each piece now sweeps out a washer: a disc with a hole. If $R$ and $r$ are the distances from the axis to the farther and the nearer curve, the washer has volume $\pi (R^2 - r^2)\,\Delta x$, and

\[ V = \pi \int_a^b \left(R(x)^2 - r(x)^2\right) dx. \]

Below, $f(x) = x + 1$ and $g(x) = \sin x$ on $[1, 3.8]$ are turned about the line $y = -1$ (the dashed red line). Change the axis in the menu: a horizontal line $y = c$ gives washers, a vertical line $x = c$ gives shells.

The shell method

Now turn the region about the $y$-axis instead (with $0 \le a < b$). Cut $[a,b]$ into $n$ pieces at the points $x_0 = a < x_1 < \dots < x_n = b$. The $k$-th strip, of height $f(m_k)$, sweeps out a cylindrical shell: a tube with inner radius $x_{k-1}$, outer radius $x_k$ and height $f(m_k)$. Its volume is

\[ \pi\left(x_k^2 - x_{k-1}^2\right) f(m_k) = 2\pi\, m_k f(m_k)\,\Delta x, \]

because $x_k^2 - x_{k-1}^2 = (x_k + x_{k-1})(x_k - x_{k-1}) = 2 m_k\,\Delta x$. Adding the shells gives a Riemann sum for $2\pi x f(x)$, so

\[ V = 2\pi \int_a^b x\, f(x)\,dx. \]

For $f(x) = 2x - x^2$ on $[0,2]$ (the example above), $V = 2\pi \int_0^2 x(2x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{8\pi}{3} \approx 8.378$. In every picture, the axis menu switches between turning about a horizontal line (discs and washers) and a vertical line (shells), so you can compare the two ways of cutting the same region.
